What you are looking at

DRONE WATCH is a homemade, listen-only drone detector. It picks up the Remote ID that most drones must broadcast, notices drone-maker Wi-Fi and FPV video links, and hears propellers with four microphones. On the left is the unit, running its real firmware in your browser. On the right is the field around it, with drones you can fly.

  1. Pick a scenario from Load a scenario, or add drones with the colored buttons.
  2. Watch the unit's HOME screen, its four lights and the buzzer (turn your sound on) as drones come into range.
  3. Click a drone on the map to select it, then click anywhere to send it there, or use fly at me and fly away.
  4. Click the unit's screen, then use Space to change screens and the arrow keys to pick a contact. Enter opens its details.

The screen, LEDs, buzzer and serial output come from the unmodified firmware (S3 v26, C5 v3), compiled to WebAssembly with mocks for the ESP32 hardware. Around it: Remote ID beacon and NAN frames, drone-maker Wi-Fi beacons and Wi-Fi FPV traffic, all with path loss, shadowing and a receiver floor. These only reach the C5 on the channel it is tuned to at that moment. The four mics get sound synthesized per sample: blade-pass harmonics with Doppler and RPM wobble, prop noise, wind, an AC unit and claps. Each sound arrives at each mic with its true delay, and the array has a 1-sample controller skew and slightly uneven arms. Detection ranges depend on these models, so treat them as a guide and check them in the field.
